## The short version

A dominant chord's tension lives in its tritone. Swap the root a tritone away and the
tension stays intact while the bass line moves somewhere new.

Take a plain **ii V I** in C:

```
Dm7  G7  Cmaj7
```

Replace `G7` with `Db7` and you get a chromatic descent into the tonic:

```
Dm7  Db7  Cmaj7
```

Both chords share `F` and `B` — the tritone that wants to resolve.

### Where it always works

1. Turnarounds at the end of a chorus.
2. Approaching a IV chord in a blues.
3. Any time a bass player wants half-step motion.

Play it slowly, sing the guide tones, and it stops being theory.
